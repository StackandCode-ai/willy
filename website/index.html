Voice assistant · runs on your own server

Say it once. Willy finds the right machine.

Willy listens on your PC and your phone and thinks on a server you control. Ask for a file, a reminder, a call or a server check, and the device that can do it does it.

Install Willy See what it can do

Free and open source. Your commands, files and devices stay on your own server.

Install it in three steps

Start with the server: the PC and phone apps ask for its address. You need a Linux machine that stays on, such as a small VPS, a Raspberry Pi or an old laptop.

Set up the server

Linux · Python 3.10 or newer

$ git clone https://github.com/StackandCode-ai/willy.git
$ cd willy && ./install
  1. It asks which AI to use (Groq, Gemini, OpenAI, OpenRouter or your own) and tests your key. A domain for HTTPS is optional.
  2. It prints a link that signs you in as the owner. The dashboard then shows a code for each phone and PC.

Add your Windows PC

Windows 10 or 11

  1. Download and unzip.
  2. Run WillyPC.exe and enter your server address and the code from your dashboard.
Download for Windows

Add your Android phone

Android phone

  1. Download the app and allow installing from your browser when asked.
  2. Open Settings in Willy, then Sign in with a code: enter the server address and the code from your dashboard.
Download the Android app

Already running Willy? Say “Hey Willy” near your PC, or hold the mic in the app. Add more devices any time from Set up on your dashboard.

One request, the right device

You don’t pick a device. Willy works out which one a request belongs to and tells you what happened.

On your PC

“Hey Willy, open VLC on screen two.”

  • Open and close apps, move windows between screens
  • Volume, brightness, per-app mute, screenshots
  • Find files, open them, send them to your phone
  • Git: status, pull, switch branch, commit and push
  • Wake word that works offline, trained on your voice

On your phone

“Call Mum.” “Set an alarm for 6.”

  • Calls, SMS and WhatsApp drafts (you tap Send)
  • Alarms, timers, reminders and a morning briefing call
  • Take a photo, open apps, start navigation
  • Ring your phone when you can’t find it
  • Push notifications when the app is asleep

On your server

“How is the server doing?”

  • CPU, memory and disk now, with history graphs
  • Sites and apps up or down, logs, restarts
  • OS updates, services, storage clean-up
  • Files and a terminal, behind a confirmation
  • Alerts on your phone and PC when something breaks

Also: a voice call screen on web and phone that pops up files, screenshots and the camera as you ask, and a dashboard that shows every device live.

Your server. Your devices. Your data.

You install the hub, so you decide who connects. Here is exactly what reaches truewilly.com, and how to stop it.

Your commands never leave your server

Commands, replies, files, reminders and API keys stay on the hub you installed. The apps talk to your hub, not to ours.

A key for every device

Each PC, phone and server gets its own key. Lose a phone and you remove that one device from the dashboard.

It asks before it changes things

Anything that deletes, installs, restarts or sends needs you to say yes first. Messages are drafted for you to send.

What we do see

If you sign in with Google, truewilly.com records your name and email. Hubs also check in once a day with their address, version, device counts, the name of the AI provider and counts of which features are used. Turn the check-in off with one setting; email sign-in never touches us.

Sign in your way

Use Google (nothing to set up), an email and password, or the private link the installer prints. Friends and family can each get their own private space on your hub.

Questions

Does it cost anything?

Willy is free. The AI runs on a key from the provider you choose, and Groq and Google Gemini both have free tiers that are enough for personal use. A server can be a machine you already own.

Which AI can I use?

Groq, Google Gemini, OpenAI, OpenRouter, or any service with an OpenAI-compatible address, including models running on your own machine with Ollama. You pick it in the installer and can change it later in the dashboard. Talking to Willy by voice needs a Groq or OpenAI key for speech-to-text, which you can add next to a different main AI.

What does truewilly.com know about me?

Only what is listed under “What we do see” above: your name and email if you use Google sign-in, plus the daily check-in unless you turn it off. Never your commands, files, replies or keys.

I don’t use Google. Can I still sign in?

Yes. Create an account with an email and password on your own hub, or use the private link the installer prints. Neither involves truewilly.com.

Do I need a domain name?

No. Without one, the apps connect to your server’s IP address over your network. A domain gives you HTTPS and lets you reach Willy from anywhere, and the installer sets that up if you give it one.

Can I run it without a server?

Willy needs a hub to connect your devices. It can run on the same Windows PC you use, but a small always-on Linux machine is the best fit because reminders and alerts keep working when your PC sleeps.

Is there an iPhone app?

Not yet. The phone app is Android only. The dashboard and the voice call page work in any browser, including on iPhone.

Will Willy send messages by itself?

No. It opens the message ready to go and you tap Send. Deleting files, installing updates, restarting services and similar changes also wait for your yes.

How do I update it?

On the server run git pull && ./install. Your settings are kept. For the apps, download the newest version from the releases page.
